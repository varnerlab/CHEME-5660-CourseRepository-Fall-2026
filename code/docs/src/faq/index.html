Model purpose & evidenceL4b, L5a

What can a price model help us decide?

A model gives probabilities for a specified decision. We can translate a required gain into a terminal-price threshold, calculate the probability of exceeding it, and examine how assumptions change that answer.

Model purpose & evidenceL3b, L4b, L5a

Why study models that miss important market behavior?

A simple model connects assumptions with consequences we can calculate and test. Its forecasts become useful when we state the event clearly and compare issued probabilities with later outcomes.

Growth & volatilityL4b

How do mean growth and drift differ?

Drift controls the growth of the expected price. Mean growth describes expected log growth per unit time. The half-variance correction connects these two averages under GBM.

Growth & volatilityL3a, L4b

How do growth rate, log return, and volatility differ?

They describe a price change or its variability on different time scales. Dividing log return by the observation interval gives a growth rate; converting its standard deviation to GBM volatility also uses the time step.

Growth & volatilityL3a

If volatility cannot predict direction, why is it useful?

Volatility describes the range of outcomes we should consider. Movement size changes the probability of crossing a loss or profit threshold even when we cannot determine the next direction.

Growth & volatilityL3a

What persists during a volatile period?

Volatility clustering concerns persistence in movement size. Large gains and large losses can occur in the same turbulent period, so evidence of clustering can inform risk without determining the next direction.

Lattices & pricingL3b

Why does one tree use two probabilities?

The real-world probability describes forecast frequencies. The risk-neutral weight prices a payoff through a replication argument. They answer different questions on the same price tree.

Lattices & pricingL3b

How do we construct a lattice from observations?

We estimate up and down price factors from observed movements, and the up probability from their frequency. Ordered paths that reach the same terminal price contribute to that node together.

Lattices & pricingL4a

Which lattice prices beat a trading target?

First translate the required scaled NPV into a sale-price threshold. Then add the real-world probabilities of terminal nodes whose prices strictly exceed it.

Dependence & covarianceL5b

How do independent inputs produce correlated movements?

The assets share random inputs through a loading matrix. Multiplying independent standard normal draws by a matrix A produces covariance AAᵀ, so the loadings determine how the assets move together.

Dependence & covarianceL5b

How do observations become a covariance matrix?

Center each asset’s growth-rate observations, multiply deviations from matching dates, and average with the sample denominator. The observation interval then connects growth-rate covariance to the covariance needed in a GBM step.

Dependence & covarianceL5b

When do independent single-asset models agree with MA-GBM?

With matching individual parameters, the models agree in their joint distribution when the cross-asset covariances are zero. Joint normality supplies the connection between zero covariance and independence here.

Estimation & uncertaintyL4b

What does a fitted log-price line predict?

The fitted line predicts log price at an observation time. Exponentiating gives a fitted price trend; interpreting it as an expected price under GBM requires the volatility correction.

Estimation & uncertaintyL5a

What changes when another price observation arrives?

An exponential moving average updates the growth-rate mean and variance. Its half-life controls the influence of older observations, while the forecast horizon specifies how far ahead the resulting model is used.

Estimation & uncertaintyL4b, L5a

Does a 95% band contain 95% of complete price paths?

A pointwise interval concerns the price at one specified date. Remaining inside a band at every date is a different event, so whole-path coverage requires its own calculation.

Estimation & uncertaintyL6a

What uncertainty does a Monte Carlo simulation measure?

The spread of simulated wealth describes modeled investment outcomes. The standard error of a simulated mean or probability measures the numerical uncertainty from using finitely many paths.

Estimation & uncertaintyL6b

What do alpha, beta, and residuals tell us?

The single index model separates an asset’s fitted response to market growth from its residual. Alpha is an intercept, beta measures sensitivity to market growth, and a residual records the observation’s unexplained part.

Estimation & uncertaintyL6b

How uncertain are the fitted alpha and beta?

Standard errors describe variation in coefficient estimates across repeated datasets under an error model. A bootstrap constructs and refits synthetic datasets to make that variation visible.

Portfolios & decisionsL5b

What stays fixed when we buy and hold?

The initial allocation determines the share counts, which stay fixed in buy-and-hold trading. Current wealth fractions change as prices move, and the exact wealth calculation follows those fixed shares.

Portfolios & decisionsL6a

How do individual growth rates become portfolio risk?

For the course’s linear growth-rate proxy, weights combine asset means and the covariance matrix combines their variances and cross terms. Correlation affects portfolio risk through those cross terms.

Portfolios & decisionsL6a

Why mix assets when one has a smaller variance?

An allocation can have less variance than either asset alone because each variance is multiplied by its squared weight and covariance determines the cross terms. The GMV calculation finds the least-variance allocation under its constraints.

Portfolios & decisionsL6a

How do exact growth targets differ from growth floors?

An exact target requires equality. A floor accepts any allocation that meets or exceeds the required mean growth, so it can retain a minimum-variance portfolio whose mean already exceeds the floor.

Portfolios & decisionsL4b, L5a

How does an NPV target become a normal probability?

Translate the discounted-profit target into a price threshold, substitute the GBM price equation, and standardize. The probability to the right of that normal threshold is the model probability of exceeding the target at the scheduled sale date.

Portfolios & decisionsL3a

Why does daily leverage not multiply the long-run return?

The daily multiple applies before each day’s result compounds. A benchmark can return to its starting price while a product targeting twice its daily return finishes below its starting value.

Portfolios & decisionsL6a

How do portfolio weights become wealth and NPV?

Use the initial dollar allocation and prices to determine share counts, then value those shares at later prices. NPV discounts terminal wealth before comparing it with the initial investment.

Dependence & covarianceL6b

Which covariance does the single index model keep or omit?

The SIM keeps covariance generated by shared market exposure. Its diagonal residual approximation omits cross-asset residual covariance, which can make estimated portfolio risk higher or lower than the sample-covariance result.

Portfolios & decisionsL6b

What changes when we lend or borrow?

The risk-free fraction controls exposure to the risky portfolio. Lending reduces that exposure. When the risky portfolio has positive excess mean growth, borrowing raises both expected growth and risk.

Portfolios & decisionsL6b

Which weights describe the risky fund and the whole investment?

Normalized risky weights describe the composition of the risky fund. Complete-investment weights also specify how much wealth is lent or borrowed at the risk-free rate.

Cash flows & bondsL1b

Why discount cash flows, and what does positive NPV mean?

Discounting expresses dated cash flows on a common valuation date. Positive NPV means the present value of the future net cash flows exceeds the initial cost at the stated benchmark rate.

Cash flows & bondsL2a

How do coupon payments and yield determine a bond price?

The coupon rate sets the promised coupon payments. The valuation yield discounts those payments and par to the present, so the payment schedule and yield play different roles.

Cash flows & bondsL2b

Why does a higher yield lower a bond’s price?

With promised payments fixed, a higher yield discounts every payment more heavily. Duration and convexity summarize the local sensitivity of the resulting price.
