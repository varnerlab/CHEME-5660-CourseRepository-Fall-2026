What does the temperature do in the Boltzmann choice rule?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The temperature sets how much the gap between an agent’s scores matters. If the temperature is much smaller than the gap, the agent almost always uses its best policy. If it is much larger, the agent picks almost at random. In between, the agent usually uses its best policy and sometimes uses the others.

2 Worked explanation

How are the policies scored? In the minority game, agent ii holds JJ fixed policies. After each round, every policy gets a new score, whether the agent used it or not:

Uij,t+1=Uij,t−πij(μt)AtP U_{ij,t+1}=U_{ij,t}-\frac{\pi_{ij}(\mu_{t})A_{t}}{P}

A policy that chose the side opposite to the attendance AtA_{t} would have put the agent in the minority, so its score goes up. For example, with P=64P=64 histories and At=+41A_{t}=+41 (41 more buyers than sellers), each policy that said sell gains 41/64=0.6441/64=0.64, and each policy that said buy loses 0.640.64. A high score means the policy has often been on the winning side.

How does the temperature turn scores into choices? The Boltzmann rule gives the probability that the agent uses policy jj:

pj=1𝒵exp(UjT)where𝒵=∑k=1Jexp(UkT) p_{j}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\exp\left(\frac{U_{j}}{T}\right)\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{k=1}^{J}\exp\left(\frac{U_{k}}{T}\right)

Only score differences matter. With J=2J=2 policies and a gap Δ=Ubest−Uother≥0\Delta=U_{\text{best}}-U_{\text{other}}\geq 0, divide the top and bottom by exp(Ubest/T)\exp(U_{\text{best}}/T):

pbest=11+exp(−Δ/T) p_{\text{best}}=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-\Delta/T)}

If Δ/T\Delta/T is large, exp(−Δ/T)\exp(-\Delta/T) is near 0, and pbestp_{\text{best}} is near 1: the agent is greedy. If Δ/T\Delta/T is near 0, exp(−Δ/T)\exp(-\Delta/T) is near 1, and pbestp_{\text{best}} is near 1/2=1/J1/2=1/J: the agent chooses at random.

An example. In the thermal minority game example, one agent ends the greedy game with a gap of Δ=4.84\Delta=4.84 between its two scores:

A temperature is only low or high relative to the score gap. At the same TT, an agent with a large gap is almost certain, and an agent with a small gap is almost undecided.

What does TT mean for real traders? One reading: TT measures how much a trader trusts the past scores of its policies. At a low TT, it always uses the policy with the best score. At a high TT, it mostly ignores the scores. In between, it usually uses the best policy but sometimes tries another, for example because a short record is a noisy guide. The optional maximum-entropy derivation makes this precise: for a given expected score, the Boltzmann rule spreads the choice as evenly as possible.

How do we choose TT? In the course, TT is a model setting that we vary. We do not estimate it from market data. The example tries values of TT from 0.010.01 to 100,000100{,}000 and measures the volatility at each. The limit T→∞T\to\infty is a baseline in which the scores have no effect. The results are in why a middle temperature gives the lowest volatility.

Try it. An agent has two policies with scores 2.02.0 and 0.50.5. What is the probability that it uses the better one at T=1.5T=1.5 and at T=15T=15? A second agent has a gap of 3.03.0. What is its probability at T=1.5T=1.5?
