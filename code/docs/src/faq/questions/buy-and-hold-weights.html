What stays fixed when we buy and hold?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The initial allocation determines the share counts, which stay fixed in buy-and-hold trading. Current wealth fractions change as prices move, and the exact wealth calculation follows those fixed shares.

2 Worked explanation

What stays fixed when we buy and hold? We choose initial dollar fractions, use them to buy shares, and keep those share counts. The fraction of current wealth in each asset then changes with its price.

How do weights become wealth? Allocate the fraction wiw_i of initial wealth W0W_0 to asset ii, buying ni=wiW0/Si(0)n_i=w_iW_0/S_i(0) shares. Holding those shares fixed gives:

Wt=∑iniSi(t)=W0∑iwiSi(t)Si(0). W_t=\sum_i n_iS_i(t)=W_0\sum_iw_i\frac{S_i(t)}{S_i(0)}.

If two equal allocations experience price factors 1.20 and 0.90, the wealth factor is 0.5(1.20)+0.5(0.90)=1.050.5(1.20)+0.5(0.90)=1.05. The first asset now accounts for 0.5(1.20)/1.05=57.14%0.5(1.20)/1.05=57.14\% of wealth and the second for 42.86%. Restoring equal fractions would require selling some of the first asset and buying more of the second.

Why do portfolio calculations use a weighted growth rate? Over one short interval, substitute the asset price factors egiΔte^{g_i\Delta t} into the wealth ratio:

1Δtln(WΔt/W0)=1Δtln(∑iwiegiΔt)≈∑iwigi=gp. \frac{1}{\Delta t}\ln(W_{\Delta t}/W_0) =\frac{1}{\Delta t}\ln\left(\sum_iw_ie^{g_i\Delta t}\right) \approx\sum_iw_ig_i=g_p.

The approximation uses ex≈1+xe^x\approx1+x, ln(1+x)≈x\ln(1+x)\approx x, and weights summing to one. It is useful for small interval changes; the wealth formula supplies the exact buy-and-hold calculation. For these price factors, the exact portfolio log return is ln(1.05)=0.04879\ln(1.05)=0.04879. The weighted asset log returns give [ln(1.20)+ln(0.90)]/2=0.03848[\ln(1.20)+\ln(0.90)]/2=0.03848. The difference shows why we use the exact wealth formula for the investment actually held.

What does a Dirichlet draw choose? Dirichlet sampling generates candidate long-only weight vectors. Equal concentration parameters of one give uniform sampling over the simplex, while larger equal concentrations favor weights near equal allocation. Comparing sampled portfolios explores possible allocations; optimization in L6a determines the minimum for the specified constraints.

Try it. With two assets and 𝛂=(1,1)\boldsymbol\alpha=(1,1), must every draw give (0.5,0.5)(0.5,0.5)?

Show the answer
no. The distribution samples all splits along the allocation line uniformly; a draw such as (0.8,0.2)(0.8,0.2) is an 80%/20% investment. Concentration controls which allocations we explore, while the price data determine their estimated reward and risk.
