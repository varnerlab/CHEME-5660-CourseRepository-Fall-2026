How does the dealer in the Wolfram market set the price, and why do traders pay a different price than the quote?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The dealer quotes its cash divided by its shares. When traders sell more shares than they buy, the dealer takes the extra shares and pays cash, keeping its cash times its shares constant, so the quote falls. All shares in that step trade at one execution price, which lies between the quotes before and after the trade. A dealer with more shares moves its quote less for the same net trade.

2 Worked explanation

How does a net sale move the quote? The dealer holds cash WtW_{t} and shares QtQ_{t} and quotes St=Wt/QtS_{t}=W_{t}/Q_{t}. Buy and Sell orders are matched with each other at the same price, and the dealer takes the other side of what is left over, the net trade xtx_{t} (shares bought minus shares sold). Its shares become Qt=Qt−1−xtQ_{t}=Q_{t-1}-x_{t}, and it keeps WtQt=Wt−1Qt−1W_{t}Q_{t}=W_{t-1}Q_{t-1}.

Take a dealer with Qt−1=100Q_{t-1}=100 shares and Wt−1=10,000W_{t-1}=10{,}000 USD, so St−1=100S_{t-1}=100 USD/share. Suppose traders sell 10 more shares than they buy, xt=−10x_{t}=-10:

Qt=100−(−10)=110the dealer takes the 10 sharesWt=10,000⋅100110=9,090.91USDcash times shares stays at 1,000,000St=9,090.91110=82.64USD/sharethe new quote \begin{aligned} Q_{t}&=100-(-10)=110 &&\text{the dealer takes the 10 shares}\\ W_{t}&=10{,}000\cdot\dfrac{100}{110}=9{,}090.91\ \text{USD} &&\text{cash times shares stays at }1{,}000{,}000\\ S_{t}&=\dfrac{9{,}090.91}{110}=82.64\ \text{USD/share} &&\text{the new quote} \end{aligned}

The quote falls by 17.4%.

What price do the sellers get? The dealer paid 10,000−9,090.91=909.0910{,}000-9{,}090.91=909.09 USD for 10 shares, so each share traded at the execution price

Ft=Wt−Wt−1xt=Wt−1Qt=10,000110=90.91USD/share F_{t}=\frac{W_{t}-W_{t-1}}{x_{t}}=\frac{W_{t-1}}{Q_{t}}=\frac{10{,}000}{110}=90.91\ \text{USD/share}

The quote is the price for a very small trade. As the dealer takes on shares, its quote slides down, so the 10 shares trade at one price between the starting quote, 100, and the ending quote, 82.64. That price is the geometric mean of the two quotes. Using St=Wt−1Qt−1/Qt2S_{t}=W_{t-1}Q_{t-1}/Q_{t}^{2}:

St−1St=Wt−1Qt−1⋅Wt−1Qt−1Qt2=(Wt−1Qt)2=Ft2 S_{t-1}S_{t}=\frac{W_{t-1}}{Q_{t-1}}\cdot\frac{W_{t-1}Q_{t-1}}{Q_{t}^{2}}=\left(\frac{W_{t-1}}{Q_{t}}\right)^{2}=F_{t}^{2}

and 100×82.64=90.91\sqrt{100\times 82.64}=90.91.

How does the dealer’s depth change the drop? Give the dealer ten times as many shares and as much cash: Qt−1=1,000Q_{t-1}=1{,}000 and Wt−1=100,000W_{t-1}=100{,}000 USD. The quote is still 100 USD/share, and the net sale is still 10 shares. The last column uses the liquidity λt=Qt−1/2\lambda_{t}=Q_{t-1}/2:

Dealer’s shares New quote Execution price Change in lnS\ln S xt/λtx_{t}/\lambda_{t}
100 82.64 90.91 −0.191-0.191 −0.200-0.200
1,000 98.03 99.01 −0.0199-0.0199 −0.0200-0.0200

Ignoring the noise, the quote follows St=St−1(Qt−1/(Qt−1−xt))2S_{t}=S_{t-1}\left(Q_{t-1}/(Q_{t-1}-x_{t})\right)^{2}. The same 10 shares are a smaller part of a deeper dealer’s shares, so the quote moves less. When the net trade is small compared with Qt−1Q_{t-1}, the change in the log price is close to xt/λtx_{t}/\lambda_{t}, the same form as in Farmer–Joshi. The dealer starts with Q0=hNtrQ_{0}=hN_{\text{tr}} shares, so a larger depth hh means smaller price moves.

When does an intention become a trade? Each Buy or Sell intention is an order for one share, and Hold sends no order. A Sell order fills only if the trader has a share. A Buy order fills only if the trader has enough cash to pay FtF_{t}. A larger net purchase raises FtF_{t}, so fewer buyers can pay. The dealer finds the largest net trade that enough traders can still complete. Orders that do not fill do not move the price. When the sellers run out of shares, the price stops falling, even if most traders still intend to sell.

Does the grid set the price, or does the price change the grid? Both, through different traders. All traders’ orders, interior and edge, set the price through the dealer. The price affects the grid only through the technical traders on the edge, and only when they are active. They compare the price with their band μt±kjσt\mu_{t}\pm k_{j}\sigma_{t}, where μt=μt−1+α(St−μt−1)\mu_{t}=\mu_{t-1}+\alpha\left(S_{t}-\mu_{t-1}\right) is a moving average of the dealer’s price. Their intentions then enter their neighbors’ sums. When the edge traders are held, the price has no effect on the grid.

Try it. A dealer holds 200 shares and 20,000 USD, and the traders buy a net 10 shares. Find the new quote and the execution price.
