Why discount cash flows, and what does positive NPV mean?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

Discounting expresses cash flows from different dates as values on one common date, so we can compare and add them. A positive NPV means that, at the stated benchmark rate, the present value of the future net cash flows exceeds the initial cost.

2 Worked explanation

Why is receiving 110 USD next year worth less than receiving it today? Suppose our benchmark pays 5% with annual compounding. An investment of 100 USD today becomes 105 USD in one year. To find what a promised 110 USD is worth today, we reverse that calculation: 110/1.05=104.76110/1.05=104.76 USD. Discounting asks how much we would need to invest today, at the benchmark rate, to reach the future payment.

How do the cash-flow symbols become a value? At date jj, we multiply each cash amount cj,kc_{j,k} by its direction νj,k\nu_{j,k} (positive for a receipt, negative for a payment) and add the results. We then discount that date’s net cash flow to time zero:

c‾j=∑k=1mjcj,kνj,k,NPV0=∑j=0N𝒟j,0−1c‾j. \bar c_j=\sum_{k=1}^{m_j}c_{j,k}\nu_{j,k},\qquad \operatorname{NPV}_0=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\mathcal D_{j,0}^{-1}\bar c_j.

The first sum combines the cash flows that occur on the same date. The second adds their present values across dates. Paying 100 USD today and receiving 110 USD in one year therefore gives NPV0=−100+110/1.05≈4.76\operatorname{NPV}_0=-100+110/1.05\approx4.76 USD. The positive result means that the future payment beats the benchmark requirement by 4.76 USD in today’s money. The discount factor is dimensionless, so each term in the NPV retains units of USD.

What if there are two cash flows on the sale date? Receiving 110 USD and paying a 2 USD fee gives c‾1=110(+1)+2(−1)=108\bar c_1=110(+1)+2(-1)=108 USD. We discount the net amount: −100+108/1.05=2.86-100+108/1.05=2.86 USD. This shows the role of each sum: the inner sum nets the cash flows on one date, and the outer sum discounts each date’s net amount.

Try it. Keep the 110 USD receipt, with no fee, but raise the benchmark to 10%. What is the NPV?

Show the answer
NPV becomes −100+110/1.10=0-100+110/1.10=0. The cash gain is still 10 USD, but it now only matches the benchmark. When future receipts are uncertain, this calculation values only the cash-flow scenario we supply.
