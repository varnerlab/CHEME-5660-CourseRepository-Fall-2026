How do individual growth rates become portfolio risk?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

In the course’s linear growth-rate proxy, the weights average the asset means, and the covariance matrix combines the asset variances with their cross terms. Correlation affects portfolio risk through those cross terms.

2 Worked explanation

How do individual growth rates become portfolio reward and risk? We use fixed weights to form the linear growth proxy gp=𝐰⊤𝐠g_p=\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf g. Its expected value measures reward, and its variance measures risk. The L6a Dirichlet example compares sampled allocations this way; the data example chooses weights by optimization.

Estimate the inputs. Each entry of 𝐆\mathbf G comes from a pair of consecutive prices on aligned dates: gi,k=ln[Si(tk)/Si(tk−1)]/Δtg_{i,k}=\ln[S_i(t_k)/S_i(t_{k-1})]/\Delta t. We average each column to get its mean, then average the products of two columns’ centered observations to get each covariance:

gi′=1N∑k=1Ngi,k,Σ̂g,ij=1N−1∑k=1N(gi,k−gi′)(gj,k−gj′). g_i^{\prime}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{i,k},\qquad \hat\Sigma_{g,ij}=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(g_{i,k}-g_i^{\prime})(g_{j,k}-g_j^{\prime}).

The diagonal entries are the individual variances. The off-diagonal entries record whether two assets tend to be above or below their own means at the same time.

Work through two assets. Suppose the estimated means are (0.06,0.12)yr−1(0.06,0.12)\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, their growth-rate standard deviations are (2,4)yr−1(2,4)\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, and their correlation is 0.250.25, so the covariance is 0.25(2)(4)=2yr−20.25(2)(4)=2\,\mathrm{yr}^{-2}. For weights (0.75,0.25)(0.75,0.25):

𝚺̂g=[42216]yr−2,𝔼̂[gp]=0.75(0.06)+0.25(0.12)=0.075yr−1,Var̂(gp)=0.752(4)+0.252(16)+2(0.75)(0.25)(2)=4yr−2. \begin{aligned} \hat{\mathbf\Sigma}_g&=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&16\end{bmatrix}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-2},\\ \widehat{\mathbb E}[g_p]&=0.75(0.06)+0.25(0.12)=0.075\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1},\\ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(g_p)&=0.75^2(4)+0.25^2(16)+2(0.75)(0.25)(2) =4\,\mathrm{yr}^{-2}. \end{aligned}

The portfolio standard deviation is 2yr−12\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. For Δt=1/252\Delta t=1/252 yr, the corresponding diffusion-volatility scale is Δtσg,p=0.126yr−1/2\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\sigma_{g,p}=0.126\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1/2}. The examples use this conversion when comparing growth-rate variability with GBM volatility.

Try it. Keep the weights and individual variances fixed, but set the correlation to zero. What changes?

Show the answer
mean growth stays 0.075yr−10.075\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, while variance falls to 3.25yr−23.25\,\mathrm{yr}^{-2} and standard deviation to 1.803yr−11.803\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. The smaller covariance reduces portfolio risk.
