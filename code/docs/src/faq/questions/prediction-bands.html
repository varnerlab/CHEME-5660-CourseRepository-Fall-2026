Does a 95% band contain 95% of complete price paths?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

No. A pointwise interval describes the price at one specified date. Staying inside the band on every date is a different event, so whole-path coverage requires its own calculation.

2 Worked explanation

A pointwise 95% prediction interval describes the model’s price distribution at one specified future date. Staying inside the interval on every date is a different event.

With the fitted GBM parameters held fixed, the bounds at elapsed time τ>0\tau>0 are:

Lz(τ)=S0eμ̂gτ−zσ̂τ,Uz(τ)=S0eμ̂gτ+zσ̂τ. L_z(\tau)=S_0e^{\hat\mu_g\tau-z\hat\sigma\sqrt\tau}, \qquad U_z(\tau)=S_0e^{\hat\mu_g\tau+z\hat\sigma\sqrt\tau}.

The values z=1z=1, 1.961.96, and 2.5762.576 give approximately 68%, 95%, and 99% pointwise coverage under the fitted model. These values are standard normal quantiles, applied to the log price before we exponentiate. The price bounds are not symmetric about the expected price because the price distribution is lognormal.

An entire path has many opportunities to leave these bounds. Its prices on different dates are dependent, so we cannot obtain the whole-path probability by simply multiplying the pointwise percentages. These bands also treat the fitted parameters as known; they do not include the uncertainty in those estimates.

The lattice example builds its bands from the expectation plus or minus a multiple of the standard deviation and computes its whole-window benchmark directly. Because the lattice distribution is discrete, the familiar normal percentage is not automatically its exact pointwise coverage either. For each example, we should state the band definition and the event being scored.
