Why do we price options with q instead of our own probability?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

Our own probability tells us the most we would pay, and buyers with different beliefs get different numbers. The market quotes one premium, so it prices with one shared rule, under which the expected share price grows at the risk-free rate.

2 Worked explanation

What does our own probability give us? Suppose a share trades at S0=100S_{0}=100 USD/share and, one year from now, will be worth either uS0=120uS_{0}=120 or dS0=90dS_{0}=90 USD/share, so u=1.2u=1.2 and d=0.9d=0.9. A call with strike K=100K=100 USD/share pays 20 USD/share after an up move and nothing after a down move. With the risk-free rate gy=0.04yr−1g_{y}=0.04\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, the one-year discount factor is 𝒟1,0−1(gy)=e−0.04=0.9608\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{1,0}(g_{y})=e^{-0.04}=0.9608. As in L9a, a buyer whose real-world up probability is pp has the expected NPV:

𝔼[NPV0]=−𝒫c+𝒟1,0−1(gy)(20)p. \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{NPV}_{0}\right]=-\mathcal{P}_{c}+\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{1,0}(g_{y})\,(20)\,p.

A buyer with p=0.7p=0.7 has a non-negative expected NPV for any premium up to 0.9608(20)(0.7)=13.450.9608(20)(0.7)=13.45 USD/share. A buyer with p=0.4p=0.4 stops at 7.69 USD/share. Each number is the most that buyer would pay, and every belief gives a different one.

Which probability gives one market price? The market quotes one premium that every buyer pays, so it prices with one shared rule, the risk-neutral pricing rule ℚ\mathbb{Q}. That rule must also agree with the prices the market already quotes for the share and for risk-free lending. For a share with no dividends, this means that under ℚ\mathbb{Q} the expected share price grows at the risk-free rate. On the tree, that condition fixes the up probability:

quS0+(1−q)dS0=S0egyΔt⇒q=egyΔt−du−d=1.04081−0.91.2−0.9=0.4694. q\,uS_{0}+(1-q)\,dS_{0}=S_{0}\,e^{g_{y}\Delta t} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=\frac{e^{g_{y}\Delta t}-d}{u-d}=\frac{1.04081-0.9}{1.2-0.9}=0.4694.

Setting the expected NPV under ℚ\mathbb{Q} to zero gives the premium:

𝒫c=𝒟1,0−1(gy)𝔼ℚ[Vc]=0.9608(20)(0.4694)=9.02USD/share. \mathcal{P}_{c}=\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{1,0}(g_{y})\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[V_{c}\right]=0.9608\,(20)(0.4694)=9.02\ \mathrm{USD/share}.

Why should the share grow at the risk-free rate? Apply the rule to the share itself. Buying the share for 100 USD and selling it after one year has the expected NPV −100+𝒟1,0−1(gy)𝔼ℚ[S1]-100+\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{1,0}(g_{y})\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S_{1}] under ℚ\mathbb{Q}. This is zero only when 𝔼ℚ[S1]=100egyΔt\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S_{1}]=100\,e^{g_{y}\Delta t}, which is the condition we used to find qq. So qq is the one up probability that gives the share’s market price an expected NPV of zero, and the option premium uses the same probability. Under a buyer’s own beliefs, the share’s expected NPV is usually not zero. With p=0.7p=0.7, it is −100+0.9608(0.7⋅120+0.3⋅90)=6.65-100+0.9608\,(0.7\cdot120+0.3\cdot90)=6.65 USD/share, because this buyer expects the share to grow faster than the risk-free rate.

Why doesn’t the real chance of an up move change the premium? The drift enters only through pp. In the CRR model, uu and dd come from the volatility and the time step (L9b), and qq comes from uu, dd, gyg_{y}, and Δt\Delta t. A buyer’s forecast does not move the premium, but it decides whether the premium is a good deal for that buyer. With p=0.7p=0.7, paying 9.02 USD/share gives an expected NPV of 13.45−9.02=4.4313.45-9.02=4.43 USD/share under that buyer’s beliefs. The value q=0.4694q=0.4694 is not a forecast that 47% of moves will be up. When we forecast how likely a trade is to make money, as with the probability of profit in L10a, we return to our own beliefs ℙ\mathbb{P}.

Try it. Change pp from 0.7 to 0.5. What changes, and what stays the same?

Show the answer
The most this buyer would pay falls to 0.9608(20)(0.5)=9.610.9608(20)(0.5)=9.61 USD/share, and the share’s expected NPV under this buyer’s beliefs falls to 0.88 USD/share. The market premium stays at 9.02 USD/share, because qq depends only on uu, dd, gyg_{y}, and Δt\Delta t.
