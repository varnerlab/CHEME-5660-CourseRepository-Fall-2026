What do alpha, beta, and residuals tell us?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The single index model separates an asset’s fitted response to market growth from its residual. Alpha is an intercept, beta measures sensitivity to market growth, and a residual records the observation’s unexplained part.

2 Worked explanation

The single index model (SIM) relates an asset’s growth rate to market growth. The subscript MM means market; the number of portfolio assets is |𝒫|\lvert\mathcal P\rvert. The release uses SPY as the market proxy and fits growth rates from aligned trading days.

What does an asset’s beta tell us? The single index model separates the fitted market response from the asset-specific residual:

gi,t=αi+βigM,t+εi,t,βi=Cov(gi,gM)Var(gM)=ρiMσg,iσg,M. g_{i,t}=\alpha_i+\beta_i g_{M,t}+\varepsilon_{i,t},\qquad \beta_i=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(g_i,g_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(g_M)} =\rho_{iM}\frac{\sigma_{g,i}}{\sigma_{g,M}}.

Beta includes both correlation and the ratio of growth-rate scales. With correlation 0.60 and asset variability twice the market’s, beta is 1.20. A 0.10yr−10.10\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1} increase in market growth then raises fitted asset growth by 0.12yr−10.12\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.

Suppose αi=0.02yr−1\alpha_i=0.02\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1} and the observed market growth on a day is 0.10yr−10.10\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. The fitted asset growth is 0.02+1.2(0.10)=0.14yr−10.02+1.2(0.10)=0.14\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. If the observed asset growth is −0.06yr−1-0.06\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, its fitted residual is −0.20yr−1-0.20\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. The residual records the part of that observation left unexplained by the line.

How do we fit the line? Choose alpha and beta to minimize the squared residuals. For a full-column-rank design matrix, the lecture gives:

𝛉̂i=argmin𝛉∥𝐲−𝐗̂𝛉∥22=(𝐗̂⊤𝐗̂)−1𝐗̂⊤𝐲. \hat{\boldsymbol\theta}_i =\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\theta}\lVert\mathbf y-\hat{\mathbf X}\boldsymbol\theta\rVert_2^2 =(\hat{\mathbf X}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf X})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf X}^{\top}\mathbf y.

The equivalent sample formulas explain the fitted coefficients:

β̂i=Cov̂(gi,gM)sg,M2,α̂i=gi′−β̂igM′. \hat\beta_i=\frac{\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(g_i,g_M)}{s_{g,M}^2},\qquad \hat\alpha_i=g_i^{\prime}-\hat\beta_i g_M^{\prime}.

The intercept ensures that the fitted line passes through the sample means. The SVD example computes the same estimate as 𝐕𝚺−1𝐔⊤𝐲\mathbf V\mathbf\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf U^{\top}\mathbf y. Agreement among the three solves checks the numerical calculation.

How much variation did the fit explain? For an asset with nonzero sample variation, compare squared residuals with squared deviations from the asset’s sample mean:

R2=1−∥𝐫i∥22∑t=1N(gi,t−gi′)2. R^2=1-\frac{\lVert\mathbf r_i\rVert_2^2}{\sum_{t=1}^{N}(g_{i,t}-g_i^{\prime})^2}.

If residuals retain 40% of the original sum of squares, R2=0.60R^2=0.60. In this one-predictor regression with an intercept, R2R^2 equals squared sample correlation. It measures in-sample fit; later observations are needed to assess forecast usefulness.

Try it. Suppose the sample correlation is 0.60 and the asset’s sample standard deviation is twice the market’s. What are β̂i\hat\beta_i and R2R^2?

Show the answer
β̂i=1.20\hat\beta_i=1.20 and R2=0.36R^2=0.36. Beta measures sensitivity to market growth, while R2R^2 measures the fraction of variation explained. The client interview uses both when screening firms.

Reading the design matrix. Each row of 𝐗̂\hat{\mathbf X} pairs the number one with that day’s market growth. Multiplying by (αi,βi)⊤(\alpha_i,\beta_i)^{\top} produces the intercept plus market contribution for each day. The fitted residual vector is what remains after subtracting those fitted values from observations.

The estimation example solves the same least-squares problem by normal equations, a QR-based solve, and singular value decomposition (SVD). For the thin SVD 𝐗̂=𝐔𝚺𝐕⊤\hat{\mathbf X}=\mathbf U\mathbf\Sigma\mathbf V^{\top}, 𝐔\mathbf U is N×2N\times2, and 𝚺\mathbf\Sigma and 𝐕\mathbf V are 2×22\times2. Here 𝚺\mathbf\Sigma contains singular values; 𝚺g\mathbf\Sigma_g denotes a growth covariance. Nonzero singular values give full column rank and a unique fit.
