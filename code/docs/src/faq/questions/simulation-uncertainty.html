What uncertainty does a Monte Carlo simulation measure?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The spread of simulated wealth describes modeled investment outcomes. The standard error of a simulated mean or probability measures the numerical uncertainty from using finitely many paths.

2 Worked explanation

What does a band around simulated wealth describe? The multiple-asset GBM example holds the fitted parameters and initial allocation fixed, simulates correlated prices, and computes wealth on every path. At a given date, the paths give a distribution of possible wealth outcomes under that model.

Separate outcome spread from precision of the mean. Average the wealth values across paths, then measure their spread:

W‾t=1n∑k=1nWt(k),sW(t)=1n−1∑k=1n(Wt(k)−W‾t)2. \bar W_t=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n}W_t^{(k)},\qquad s_W(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(W_t^{(k)}-\bar W_t)^2}.

Suppose 2,000 independent paths give a mean of 1,100 USD and a standard deviation of 150 USD at a selected date. The example’s approximate 95% pointwise prediction band is:

W‾t±1.96sW(t)=1,100±294USD. \bar W_t\pm1.96s_W(t)=1{,}100\pm294\ \mathrm{USD}.

It describes the spread of wealth outcomes on that date. Its normal approximation can be imperfect because wealth is skewed. By contrast, the Monte Carlo standard error of the mean is 150/2,000=3.35150/\sqrt{2{,}000}=3.35 USD, giving an approximate 95% interval of 1,100±6.571{,}100\pm6.57 USD for the model’s expected wealth. Pointwise coverage refers to a single date; whole-path coverage is a different event.

Estimate a target probability. Discount each path’s terminal wealth to obtain ρT(k)\rho_T^{(k)}. Count the paths strictly above ρ⋆\rho_\star and divide by the number of paths:

p̂=#{k:ρT(k)>ρ⋆}n,SE(p̂)=p̂(1−p̂)n. \hat p=\frac{\#\{k:\rho_T^{(k)}>\rho_\star\}}{n},\qquad \operatorname{SE}(\hat p)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}.

If 1,360 of 2,000 paths exceed the target, p̂=0.68\hat p=0.68 and its standard error is 0.01040.0104, about one percentage point. An approximate 95% Monte Carlo interval is 0.68±0.02040.68\pm0.0204. This quantifies the effect of using finitely many random paths while keeping the model inputs fixed.

The simulation first checks that generated growth rates reproduce the fitted means and covariance. The later comparison with observed 2025 wealth asks a separate question: how well those fitted inputs describe a new period.

Try it. Increase the number of independent paths from 2,000 to 8,000. What should shrink?

Show the answer
Monte Carlo standard errors are approximately halved. The estimated spread of individual wealth outcomes remains near 150 USD because the model and allocation have not changed. More paths improve numerical precision; uncertainty in the fitted inputs requires a separate analysis.
