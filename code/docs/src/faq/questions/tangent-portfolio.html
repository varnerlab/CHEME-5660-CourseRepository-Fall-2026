Which weights describe the risky fund and the whole investment?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The normalized risky weights describe the composition of the risky fund. The complete-investment weights also specify how much wealth is lent or borrowed at the risk-free rate.

2 Worked explanation

Which weights describe the risky fund, and which describe the whole investment? With 1,000 USD of initial wealth, risky weights of (0.30,0.45)(0.30,0.45) put 300 USD in the first firm and 450 USD in the second, and the remaining 250 USD is lent at the risk-free rate. The risky weights sum to 0.750.75, so wf=0.25w_f=0.25 and the normalized risky-fund weights are (0.4,0.6)(0.4,0.6). If we instead borrowed 50% of initial wealth, the complete-portfolio risky weights would be (0.6,0.9)(0.6,0.9), with the same normalized mix.

Which risky portfolio defines the steepest capital allocation line? The tangent portfolio maximizes excess mean growth per unit standard deviation. When the covariance is positive definite, signed risky weights are allowed, and the GMV mean growth exceeds gfg_f, the lecture obtains:

𝐰𝒯=𝚺g−1𝐞𝟏⊤𝚺g−1𝐞,𝐞=𝛍g−gf𝟏. \mathbf w_{\mathcal T}=\frac{\mathbf\Sigma_g^{-1}\mathbf e}{\mathbf1^{\top}\mathbf\Sigma_g^{-1}\mathbf e}, \qquad \mathbf e=\boldsymbol\mu_g-g_f\mathbf1.

The inverse covariance adjusts the excess-growth vector for risk; the denominator makes the risky fund’s weights sum to one. Long-only restrictions require a numerical solve. The lecture’s problem (SIM-2) measures the risky weights as fractions of total initial wealth:

minimize𝐰𝐰⊤𝚺̂g,SIM𝐰subject to(𝐠̂SIM−gf𝟏)⊤𝐰≥g⋆−gf,0≤wi≤1,wf=1−𝟏⊤𝐰. \begin{aligned} \underset{\mathbf w}{\operatorname{minimize}}\quad &\mathbf w^{\top}\hat{\mathbf\Sigma}_{g,\mathrm{SIM}}\mathbf w\\ \text{subject to}\quad &(\hat{\mathbf g}_{\mathrm{SIM}}-g_f\mathbf1)^{\top}\mathbf w\geq g_\star-g_f,\\ &0\leq w_i\leq1,\qquad w_f=1-\mathbf1^{\top}\mathbf w. \end{aligned}

The risky weights can sum to more than one because borrowing is allowed. The bounds wi≤1w_i\leq1 cap each risky position at the initial wealth, so they eventually limit how far we can borrow along a fixed risky mix.

Recover the risky fund. For feasible targets g⋆>gfg_\star>g_f, and until an upper bound binds, we normalize a nonzero solution by its risky fraction. Every such target selects the same long-only tangent mix:

𝐰𝒯=𝐰𝟏⊤𝐰,𝐰(g⋆)=(1−wf)𝐰𝒯,1−wf=g⋆−gf𝐠̂SIM⊤𝐰𝒯−gf. \mathbf w_{\mathcal T}=\frac{\mathbf w}{\mathbf1^{\top}\mathbf w},\qquad \mathbf w(g_\star)=(1-w_f)\mathbf w_{\mathcal T},\qquad 1-w_f=\frac{g_\star-g_f}{\hat{\mathbf g}_{\mathrm{SIM}}^{\top}\mathbf w_{\mathcal T}-g_f}.

Follow the complete investment. The risky/risk-free example fixes the initial share counts and lets the risk-free position accumulate. With the tangent weights normalized to sum to one, wealth is:

Wt=W0[wfegft+(1−wf)∑iw𝒯,iSi(t)Si(0)]. W_t=W_0\left[w_fe^{g_ft}+(1-w_f)\sum_i w_{\mathcal T,i}\frac{S_i(t)}{S_i(0)}\right].

When wf<0w_f<0, the first term is a debt that grows over time. The example assumes one rate for both borrowing and lending. Under buy-and-hold, the risk-free and firm fractions drift, so the realized growth and risk need not equal the fixed-weight CAL predictions. The comparison uses 2025 prices that were held out of estimation and scores both tangent funds under the same sample covariance.
