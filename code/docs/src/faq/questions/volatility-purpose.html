If volatility cannot predict direction, why is it useful?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

Volatility describes the range of outcomes we should prepare for. The size of price movements changes the probability of crossing a loss or profit threshold, even when we cannot predict the direction of the next move.

2 Worked explanation

Volatility tells us how wide a range of outcomes we should be prepared for. A price distribution with a larger spread gives different probabilities of crossing a loss or profit threshold, even when we cannot determine the sign of the next move.

Volatility clustering is the tendency for large movements to occur near other large movements. We observed persistence in growth-rate magnitudes even when signed growth rates had little linear autocorrelation. We conclude that recent large movements can inform our expectation of the size of future movements. That conclusion does not guarantee another large movement, imply that gains and losses alternate, or establish a 50/50 chance of an up move.

There is no single duration after which every volatility cluster ends. We can inspect the autocorrelation of magnitudes to describe persistence in a dataset and to evaluate models that update their risk estimates. An autocorrelation plot is not a countdown to the end of the current episode, and the observed pattern alone does not identify what caused it.
