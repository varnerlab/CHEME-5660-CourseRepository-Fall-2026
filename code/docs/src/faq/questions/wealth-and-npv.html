How do portfolio weights become wealth and NPV?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

We use the initial dollar allocation and the initial prices to determine the share counts, then value those shares at later prices. NPV discounts the terminal wealth before comparing it with the initial investment.

2 Worked explanation

How do we evaluate the investment after choosing its weights? We convert the initial dollar allocations to shares, keep the share counts fixed, and value the shares at later prices. The data and multiple-asset GBM examples use the same wealth calculation on observed and simulated prices.

Work from dollars to shares. Suppose W0=1,000W_0=1{,}000 USD, weights are (0.6,0.4)(0.6,0.4), and initial share prices are (100,50)(100,50) USD. We buy six shares of the first asset and eight of the second. In symbols:

ni=wiW0Si(0),Wt=∑iniSi(t)=W0∑iwiSi(t)Si(0). n_i=\frac{w_iW_0}{S_i(0)},\qquad W_t=\sum_i n_iS_i(t)=W_0\sum_i w_i\frac{S_i(t)}{S_i(0)}.

If the prices after one year are (110,45)(110,45) USD, wealth is 6(110)+8(45)=1,0206(110)+8(45)=1{,}020 USD. The first asset now represents 660/1,020=64.7%660/1{,}020=64.7\% of wealth. Its initial fraction was 60%: the share count stayed at six while its fraction of wealth changed.

Did we exceed the benchmark? We discount terminal wealth before comparing it with the initial investment:

NPV(gy,T)=−W0+WTe−gyT,ρT=WTW0e−gyT−1. \operatorname{NPV}(g_y,T)=-W_0+W_Te^{-g_yT},\qquad \rho_T=\frac{W_T}{W_0}e^{-g_yT}-1.

For gy=0.03yr−1g_y=0.03\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1} and T=1T=1 yr, the scaled NPV is 1.02e−0.03−1≈−0.010151.02e^{-0.03}-1\approx-0.01015. Wealth rose by 20 USD, but its discounted value fell short of the initial investment by about 10.15 USD. To exceed a scaled target ρ⋆>−1\rho_\star>-1, terminal wealth must satisfy:

WT>W0(1+ρ⋆)egyT. W_T>W_0(1+\rho_\star)e^{g_yT}.

For a zero target, the required wealth is 1,030.451{,}030.45 USD. This translates the NPV question into an event defined on terminal wealth, which we can count across simulated paths.

How do we use the testing data? The data example estimates the inputs from 2014 to 2024 and evaluates both GMV allocations, equal weights, and SPY on 2025 prices. Realized mean growth is ln(WT/W0)/T\ln(W_T/W_0)/T when wealth remains positive. Realized risk is the sample standard deviation of daily wealth growth rates. These quantities describe the investment we actually held, whose weights can drift over time.

Try it. If we double W0W_0 and keep the weights and price ratios fixed, what changes?

share counts, wealth, and dollar NPV double. Scaled NPV and the probability of exceeding a scaled target stay the same. The calculation allows fractional shares and omits trading costs and dividends.
