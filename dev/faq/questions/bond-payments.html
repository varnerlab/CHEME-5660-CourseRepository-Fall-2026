How do coupon payments and yield determine a bond price?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The coupon rate sets the promised coupon payments. The valuation yield discounts those payments and par to the present, so the payment schedule and yield play different roles.

2 Worked explanation

Where does the investment gain come from? A bill held to maturity pays par once; we buy it for a lower amount in the example below. A coupon bond also pays cash along the way. In both cases, identify the dated payments first, then use the stated rate convention to connect them to today’s price.

From a bill quote to investment growth. The bank-discount quote expresses the discount as a fraction of par. We first calculate the purchase price, then use the purchase-to-par ratio to calculate growth over the holding period:

VB=VP(1−dD360),gB=1Tln(VPVB). V_B=V_P\left(1-d\frac{D}{360}\right),\qquad g_B=\frac{1}{T}\ln\left(\frac{V_P}{V_B}\right).

For VP=100V_P=100 USD, d=0.04d=0.04, and D=90D=90 days, the bill costs 99 USD. If we select T=90/365T=90/365 years for valuation, gB≈0.04076yr−1g_B\approx0.04076\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. The quote uses par and a 360-day basis; the growth calculation uses the amount invested and the selected holding-time convention.

What changes for a coupon bond? We receive coupons along the way as well as par at maturity. Just after a coupon payment, with a constant discount yield, its value is the sum of those discounted payments:

VB=VP𝒟N,0−1⏟present value of par+C∑j=1N𝒟j,0−1⏟present value of coupons. V_B=\underbrace{V_P\mathcal D_{N,0}^{-1}}_{\text{present value of par}} +\underbrace{C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal D_{j,0}^{-1}}_{\text{present value of coupons}}.

Each discount factor applies to the date of its payment. This is the L1b cash-flow calculation with the bond’s payment schedule supplied.

Price a short coupon schedule. Let par be 100 USD, the annual coupon rate be 4%, and payments occur twice per year. Each coupon is C=(0.04/2)(100)=2C=(0.04/2)(100)=2 USD. With one year remaining, the payments are 2 USD in six months and 102 USD in one year. At a 6% nominal yield compounded semiannually:

VB=21.03+1021.032=98.09USD. V_B=\frac{2}{1.03}+\frac{102}{1.03^2}=98.09\ \mathrm{USD}.

The coupon rate specifies the promised 2 USD payments. The yield discounts them. Here the coupon rate is below the yield, so the price is below par.
