How do we construct a lattice from observations?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

We estimate up and down price factors from observed movements, and the up probability from their frequency. Ordered paths that reach the same terminal price contribute to that node together.

2 Worked explanation

Our data-driven example estimates the up factor from observed positive movements, the down factor from negative movements, and pp from the frequency of up moves. Suppose four consecutive price factors are:

1.02,0.99,1.04,0.98. 1.02,\quad0.99,\quad1.04,\quad0.98.

The estimates are:

u=1.02+1.042=1.03,d=0.99+0.982=0.985,p=24=0.50. u=\frac{1.02+1.04}{2}=1.03, \qquad d=\frac{0.99+0.98}{2}=0.985, \qquad p=\frac{2}{4}=0.50.

Starting from S0=100S_0=100, a two-step lattice has three terminal states:

Up moves Terminal price Probability
2 100u2=106.09100u^2=106.09 p2=0.25p^2=0.25
1 100ud=101.455100ud=101.455 2p(1−p)=0.502p(1-p)=0.50
0 100d2=97.0225100d^2=97.0225 (1−p)2=0.25(1-p)^2=0.25

There are four ordered paths but only three terminal states: up–down and down–up reach the same price. Their probabilities must be added. Recombination follows from ud=duud=du; it does not require d=1/ud=1/u. The value p=0.50p=0.50 comes from this particular sample, not from a requirement of the model.

When the observations are stored as growth rates, recover the price factor with egkΔte^{g_k\Delta t}. This reverses the earlier logarithm and time scaling. The course estimator averages factors within each sign group; averaging the logarithms and exponentiating would be a different estimator. A zero-change observation joins neither factor group but remains in the denominator when estimating pp. We need at least one up move and one down move to estimate both factors.
