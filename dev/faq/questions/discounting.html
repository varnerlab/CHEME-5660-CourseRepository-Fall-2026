Why discount cash flows, and what does positive NPV mean?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

Discounting expresses dated cash flows on a common valuation date. Positive NPV means the present value of the future net cash flows exceeds the initial cost at the stated benchmark rate.

2 Worked explanation

Why is receiving 110 USD next year worth less than receiving it today? Suppose our benchmark pays 5% with annual compounding. An investment of 100 USD today becomes 105 USD in one year. To find today’s equivalent of a promised 110 USD, reverse that same calculation: 110/1.05=104.76110/1.05=104.76 USD. Discounting asks how much we would need today to reach the future payment at the selected benchmark.

How do the cash-flow symbols become a value? At date jj, multiply each cash amount cj,kc_{j,k} by its direction νj,k\nu_{j,k} and add the results. Then discount that date’s net cash flow to time zero:

c‾j=∑k=1mjcj,kνj,k,NPV0=∑j=0N𝒟j,0−1c‾j. \bar c_j=\sum_{k=1}^{m_j}c_{j,k}\nu_{j,k},\qquad \operatorname{NPV}_0=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\mathcal D_{j,0}^{-1}\bar c_j.

The first sum combines payments made on the same date. The second combines their present values across dates. Paying 100 USD today and receiving 110 USD in one year therefore gives NPV0=−100+110/1.05≈4.76\operatorname{NPV}_0=-100+110/1.05\approx4.76 USD. The positive result means that the payment exceeds the benchmark requirement by 4.76 USD in today’s money. The discount factor is dimensionless, so each term in the NPV retains units of USD.

What if there are two cash flows on the sale date? Receiving 110 USD and paying a 2 USD fee gives c‾1=110(+1)+2(−1)=108\bar c_1=110(+1)+2(-1)=108 USD. Discount the net amount: −100+108/1.05=2.86-100+108/1.05=2.86 USD. Combining same-date payments first explains the inner sum; discounting each date explains the outer sum.

Try it. Keep the 110 USD receipt, with no fee, but raise the benchmark to 10%.

Show the answer
NPV becomes −100+110/1.10=0-100+110/1.10=0. The cash gain is still 10 USD; it now exactly meets the benchmark. With uncertain future receipts, this calculation values the supplied cash-flow scenario.
