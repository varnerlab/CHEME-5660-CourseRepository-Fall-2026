Why does one tree use two probabilities?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The real-world probability describes how often we forecast each move to occur. The risk-neutral weight prices a payoff through a replication argument. The two answer different questions on the same price tree.

2 Worked explanation

Why does one tree use two probabilities? We ask the same tree two different questions. Suppose a share costs 100 USD today and will be worth either 120 or 90 USD after one step, so the up and down factors are u=1.20u=1.20 and d=0.90d=0.90. These prices stay fixed for both questions.

Forecasting: how often might each outcome occur? Let the estimated real-world up probability be p=0.65p=0.65. Weighting the two prices by their forecast probabilities gives:

𝔼ℙ[S1]=0.65(120)+0.35(90)=109.50USD/share. \mathbb E_{\mathbb P}[S_1]=0.65(120)+0.35(90)=109.50\ \mathrm{USD/share}.

This average summarizes the forecast distribution. It is only as good as the estimated probabilities, which we judge by how well they describe later outcomes.

Pricing: what should a specified payoff cost today? Suppose a claim pays Hu=20H_u=20 USD after an up move and Hd=0H_d=0 after a down move. Let the risk-free accumulation factor over the same step be Rf=1.02R_f=1.02. If trading is frictionless, short positions are allowed, and we can borrow or lend at this rate, the absence of arbitrage requires the risk-neutral weight qq to satisfy:

qu+(1−q)d=Rf⇒q=Rf−du−d=1.02−0.901.20−0.90=0.40. qu+(1-q)d=R_f \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=\frac{R_f-d}{u-d}=\frac{1.02-0.90}{1.20-0.90}=0.40.

The condition d<Rf<ud<R_f<u gives 0<q<10<q<1. We use these weights to average the claim’s payoffs and then discount one step:

V0=qHu+(1−q)HdRf=0.40(20)+0.60(0)1.02≈7.84USD. V_0=\frac{qH_u+(1-q)H_d}{R_f} =\frac{0.40(20)+0.60(0)}{1.02}\approx7.84\ \mathrm{USD}.

Why is that the price? We can build the claim’s payoff from the share and a loan. The claim’s payoff differs by 20−0=2020-0=20 USD between the two states, while the share price differs by 120−90=30120-90=30 USD/share. Holding 20/30=2/320/30=2/3 of a share therefore reproduces the payoff difference. In the down state that holding is worth 60 USD while the claim pays zero, so we pair it with a loan that requires a 60 USD repayment, which means borrowing 60/1.0260/1.02 USD today. After one step the holding is worth 80 or 60 USD and we repay 60 USD, leaving 20 or zero: exactly the claim’s payoff. Building this position costs 100(2/3)−60/1.02≈7.84100(2/3)-60/1.02\approx7.84 USD today. If the claim traded at any other price, we could trade it against the replicating position for an arbitrage profit under the stated assumptions.

Thus pp describes forecast frequencies, while qq values a payoff through a trading argument. Risk-neutral pricing does not assert that the stock is risk-free or that 40% of actual moves will be upward.

Try it. Change only pp from 0.65 to 0.50. What changes?

Show the answer
the forecast expected price becomes 105 USD/share. The replicated payoff still costs about 7.84 USD because the two possible share prices and the borrowing rate are unchanged.
