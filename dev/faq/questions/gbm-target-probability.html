How does an NPV target become a normal probability?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

Translate the discounted-profit target into a price threshold, substitute the GBM price equation, and standardize. The probability to the right of that normal threshold is the model probability of exceeding the target at the scheduled sale date.

2 Worked explanation

First specify the trade. We buy at S0S_0 and sell at the scheduled time TT, ignoring dividends, fees, and other trading costs. With a constant benchmark growth rate gyg_y, the scaled NPV is:

ρT=e−gyTSTS0−1. \rho_T=e^{-g_yT}\frac{S_T}{S_0}-1.

The benchmark need not be risk-free; it represents the growth requirement used in this comparison. We ask whether ρT\rho_T exceeds a target ρ⋆>−1\rho_\star>-1.

Substitute the GBM solution, add one, and take the logarithm. The event becomes:

(μg−gy)T+σTZ>ln(1+ρ⋆). (\mu_g-g_y)T+\sigma\sqrt T\,Z>\ln(1+\rho_\star).

For T>0T>0 and σ>0\sigma>0, divide by the positive quantity σT\sigma\sqrt T. This gives a threshold for the standard normal variable:

Z>z⋆,z⋆=ln(1+ρ⋆)−(μg−gy)TσT,ℙ(ρT>ρ⋆)=1−Φ(z⋆). Z>z_\star, \qquad z_\star=\frac{\ln(1+\rho_\star)-(\mu_g-g_y)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}, \qquad \mathbb P(\rho_T>\rho_\star)=1-\Phi(z_\star).

For example, take μg=0.06\mu_g=0.06, gy=0.02g_y=0.02, σ=0.20\sigma=0.20, and T=1T=1 in the corresponding year-based units. With target ρ⋆=0\rho_\star=0, the threshold is z⋆=−0.20z_\star=-0.20, giving probability about 0.57930.5793. We have calculated a model probability of beating the benchmark at the scheduled sale time.

A lower threshold leaves more normal probability to its right. This explains why increasing mean growth, with the other quantities fixed, raises the probability even though it lowers z⋆z_\star.

This calculation does not give the probability of reaching the target at any earlier time and selling immediately. That is a first-passage question with a different event.
