How do growth rate, log return, and volatility differ?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

They describe a price change or its variability on different time scales. Dividing log return by the observation interval gives a growth rate; converting its standard deviation to GBM volatility also uses the time step.

2 Worked explanation

We start with the same observed price change and express it in different units. For observations separated by Δt\Delta t years, the log return rkr_k and growth rate gkg_k are:

rk=ln(SkSk−1),gk=rkΔt. r_k=\ln\left(\frac{S_k}{S_{k-1}}\right), \qquad g_k=\frac{r_k}{\Delta t}.
Quantity Meaning Units when time is in years
rkr_k Log return over one observation interval Dimensionless
gkg_k Log return per unit time year−1\mathrm{year}^{-1}
σg\sigma_g Sample standard deviation of the observed growth rates year−1\mathrm{year}^{-1}
σr(Δt)\sigma_r(\Delta t) Sample standard deviation of interval log returns Dimensionless
σ̂\hat\sigma Estimated GBM volatility parameter year−1/2\mathrm{year}^{-1/2}

For a fixed observation interval, scaling every observation scales its standard deviation by the same factor. Therefore:

σr(Δt)=Δtσg. \sigma_r(\Delta t)=\Delta t\,\sigma_g.

Under the constant-parameter GBM model, growth rates have variance σ2/Δt\sigma^2/\Delta t. We recover the model’s volatility estimate using:

σ̂=Δtσg=σr(Δt)Δt. \hat\sigma=\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\sigma_g =\frac{\sigma_r(\Delta t)}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}.

For daily observations with Δt=1/252\Delta t=1/252, a growth-rate standard deviation of approximately 3.1749year−13.1749\ \mathrm{year}^{-1} gives σ̂≈0.20year−1/2\hat\sigma\approx0.20\ \mathrm{year}^{-1/2}. The same calculation starts from daily log-return standard deviation 0.0125990.012599 and multiplies it by 252\sqrt{252}.

The first conversion is an exact change of units. Using square-root-of-time scaling to forecast risk over a different horizon requires assumptions about dependence and variability. Volatility clustering is a reason to check those assumptions.

The sample mean of the gkg_k estimates μg\mu_g. Add σ̂2/2\hat\sigma^2/2 to recover μ̂\hat\mu; do not add half the unscaled variance of the annualized daily growth rates.
