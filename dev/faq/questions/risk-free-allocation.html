What changes when we lend or borrow?

  1. Short answer
  2. Worked explanation
  3. Notes and sources

1 Short answer

The risk-free fraction controls exposure to the risky portfolio. Lending reduces that exposure, and borrowing increases it. When the risky portfolio has positive excess mean growth, borrowing raises both expected growth and risk.

2 Worked explanation

We combine a risky portfolio with an asset whose growth rate is known over the holding period. The weight on the risk-free asset determines whether we lend or borrow. Here we use the course’s fixed-weight, linear growth-rate proxy; the buy-and-hold wealth calculation instead follows the actual holdings as their wealth fractions change.

What happens when we lend or borrow? Put fraction wfw_f in the risk-free asset and fraction 1−wf1-w_f in a fixed risky portfolio. The risk-free asset’s known growth contributes to the mean and adds no variance:

𝔼[gc]=gf+(1−wf)(𝔼[gp]−gf),σg,c=|1−wf|σg,p. \mathbb E[g_c]=g_f+(1-w_f)(\mathbb E[g_p]-g_f),\qquad \sigma_{g,c}=|1-w_f|\sigma_{g,p}.

For daily observations (Δt=1/252\Delta t=1/252 yr), suppose gf=0.02yr−1g_f=0.02\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, 𝔼[gp]=0.08yr−1\mathbb E[g_p]=0.08\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, and σg,p=3.0yr−1\sigma_{g,p}=3.0\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. A 50% risk-free allocation gives mean growth 0.050.05 and standard deviation 1.5yr−11.5\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. Borrowing 50% of initial wealth means wf=−0.5w_f=-0.5: mean growth becomes 0.110.11 and the standard deviation 4.5yr−14.5\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}. Borrowing increases both the risky exposure and the uncertainty.

For wf≤1w_f\leq1, eliminating the risky fraction gives the capital allocation line (CAL):

𝔼[gc]=gf+SRpσg,c. \mathbb E[g_c]=g_f+\mathrm{SR}_p\,\sigma_{g,c}.

Its slope is the risky portfolio’s Sharpe ratio, (0.08−0.02)/3=0.02(0.08-0.02)/3=0.02. Under independent-increment scaling, the annualized ratio is 252(0.02)=0.3175\sqrt{252}(0.02)=0.3175. Expressing daily growth in inverse years does not, by itself, annualize this ratio.

Try it. For a target mean growth of 0.065yr−10.065\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}, what risk-free fraction is needed?

Show the answer
1−wf=(0.065−0.02)/(0.08−0.02)=0.751-w_f=(0.065-0.02)/(0.08-0.02)=0.75, so wf=0.25w_f=0.25 and the standard deviation is 2.25yr−12.25\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.
