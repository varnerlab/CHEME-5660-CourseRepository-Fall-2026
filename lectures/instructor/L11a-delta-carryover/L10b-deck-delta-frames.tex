\begin{frame}{Delta as a Hedging Tool for Composite Contracts}
  Delta is also the number of shares that hedges one contract. A composite
  position's Delta is the sum of its legs' Deltas, so the same idea hedges the
  whole position.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{\ink{What is hedging?}} Offsetting potential losses with an opposite
  position in a related \hilite{counter asset}, often the underlying shares.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  A position with zero Delta is insensitive to small changes in the share price.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What Is the Delta of a Composite Contract?}
  We hold $\theta_{i}n_{i}$ American contracts of leg $i$, each for $m$ shares,
  and $N_{S}$ shares. With $\Pc_{i}(S,t)$ the premium of leg $i$ at share price
  $S$ and time $t$, the portfolio is worth
  $\Pi(S,t)=N_{S}S+m\sum_{i\in\Cc}\theta_{i}n_{i}\Pc_{i}(S,t)$, and the
  \hilite{net Delta} is its sensitivity to the share price:
  \begin{align*}
    \Delta_{\text{net}}(S,t)&=\frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial S}
    = \underbrace{\frac{\partial (N_{S} S)}{\partial S}}_{\textstyle=N_{S}} + m\sum_{i\in\Cc} \theta_{i} n_{i} \underbrace{\frac{\partial \Pc_{i}}{\partial S}}_{\textstyle=\Delta_{i}(S,t)}\\
    &= N_{S} + \underbrace{m\sum_{i\in\Cc} \theta_{i} n_{i} \Delta_{i}(S,t)}_{\textstyle= \Delta_{\text{opts}}(S,t)}
  \end{align*}
  The options contribute $\Delta_{\text{opts}}=m\,\Delta_{\Cc}$, where
  $\Delta_{\Cc}(S,t)=\sum_{i\in\Cc}\theta_{i}n_{i}\Delta_{i}(S,t)$ is the \hilite{composite
  Delta}, per share.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Delta-Neutral Position}
  A position is \hilite{delta-neutral} if $\Delta_{\text{net}}(S,t)=0$. We get
  there by choosing $N_{S}$ to offset the Delta of the options:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Delta_{\text{net}}(S,t)= N_{S} + \Delta_{\text{opts}}(S,t) = 0
    \quad\Rightarrow\quad
    N_{S} = - \Delta_{\text{opts}}(S,t)
  \end{equation*}
  \textbf{\ink{Quick gut-check example:}} short $10$ calls at $\Delta=0.6$ with
  $m=100$ shares/contract, so $\Delta_{\text{opts}}=10\times(-1)\times0.6\times100=-600$.
  Holding $N_{S}=+600$ shares gives $\Delta_{\text{net}}=600-600=0$.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  If leg $i$ is exercised or assigned at time $\tau\leq T$, set $\Delta_{i}=0$
  for $t\geq\tau$ and update the shares:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}
    \item \hilite{Call leg $i$}: $N_{S}\gets N_{S}+\theta_{i}\cdot m\cdot n_{i}$.
    \item \hilite{Put leg $i$}: $N_{S}\gets N_{S}-\theta_{i}\cdot m\cdot n_{i}$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Delta of the Common Composite Contracts}
  Short legs at Deltas $\mp0.25$, long legs at $\mp0.10$ beyond them, and a
  straddle at $\mp0.5$. At entry, one copy of each leg ($m=100$):
  \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{lll}
      \toprule
      Composite contract & Composite Delta, $\Delta_{\Cc}$ & Shares to hedge, $N_{S}$ \\
      \midrule
      Short put & $-(-0.25)=0.25$ & $-25$ \\
      Short call & $-0.25$ & $+25$ \\
      Put credit spread & $-0.10-(-0.25)=0.15$ & $-15$ \\
      Call credit spread & $-0.25+0.10=-0.15$ & $+15$ \\
      Short straddle & $-(-0.5)-0.5\approx0$ & $\approx0$ \\
      Short strangle & $-(-0.25)-0.25=0$ & $0$ \\
      Protected strangle & $-(-0.25)-0.25+0.10=0.10$ & $-10$ \\
      Iron condor & $-0.10-(-0.25)-0.25+0.10=0$ & $0$ \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  At these strikes, the sign of $\Delta_{\Cc}$ matches the outlook.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reading the Delta Table}
  $\Delta_{\Cc}$ is the position's gain per share when the share price rises 1
  USD/share:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5em}
    \item \hilite{The rule of thumb}: a Delta of $\pm0.25$ is roughly a
      one-in-four chance of finishing in the money under $\Q$.
    \item \hilite{The protected strangle}: its long call leaves $+0.10$, so it
      leans bullish.
    \item \hilite{Zero is not zero risk}: Gamma pulls the Delta away from zero as
      the share price moves.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  Staying delta-neutral means updating $N_{S}$ as the Deltas change.
