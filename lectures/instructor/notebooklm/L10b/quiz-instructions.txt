L10b Self-Check: The Greeks, POE, and POP

This is an ungraded self-check on what we did in L10b:

1. What each Greek measures. Divide our Theta by 365 for one day, and our Vega and Rho by 100 for one percentage point. One contract acts like 100 times Delta shares.
2. How to compute the probability of exercise (POE) and the probability of profit (POP) from simulated share prices at expiration.
3. Delta is close to the POE under the risk-neutral probabilities ℚ, but it is not our own forecast under our belief ℙ.

There are 10 multiple-choice questions, and most use the NVDA examples from class. Each answer comes with a short explanation. Take it as many times as you like; it does not count toward your grade.

Keep the lecture and the examples open while you work. If a question says ℙ, it means our belief (our forecast). If it says ℚ, it means the risk-neutral probabilities inside the market price.

The questions were written from the course materials and checked by the teaching team. If you think an answer is wrong, post it on the course discussion board.
