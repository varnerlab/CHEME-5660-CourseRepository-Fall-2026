L12b Self-Check: Why Do Markets Crash?

This is an ungraded self-check on the three core concepts from L12b:

1. A Wolfram rule is a lookup table. A trader sums its four neighbors' intentions and reads its next intention from the table. The nine entries, read from sum 8 to sum 0, give the rule number in base 3.
2. The dealer quotes the price as its cash divided by its shares. Net buying raises the price and net selling lowers it, apart from the added noise. An intention becomes a filled order only when the trader has the cash or shares needed to trade.
3. A rule-switch experiment compares a switched market with a control that shares its history and never switches. Sell intentions can spread beyond the traders whose rules change. The simulation is an analogy for a crash, not an explanation of a historical one.

There are 12 multiple-choice questions, each with a short explanation. Take it as many times as you like; it does not count toward your grade.

Keep the lecture and the two in-class examples open while you work. Every rule entry or number used in a calculation is given in the question. You will not need to reproduce the dealer's price-update formula, the technical-trader band, either algorithm, or the optional Q-learning material.
