L13a Self-Check: Synthetic Markets with Jump Hidden Markov Models

This is an ungraded self-check on the three core concepts from L13a:

1. A Markov chain is a set of states and a transition matrix whose rows sum to one. We estimate each entry by counting moves between states on consecutive days. The stationary distribution gives the long-run share of time in each state.
2. The jump hidden Markov model bins the daily growth rate into states of equal probability under a fitted Laplace distribution, estimates the transition matrix by counting, and draws each day's growth rate from a Student-t distribution of the day's state. Jump episodes start with probability epsilon and last lambda days on average. They keep volatile periods going.
3. Jump episodes add volatility clustering, but more or longer episodes thin the simulated tails.
