% Summary frames carry all three parts: one summary sentence, exactly three
% key takeaways, one concluding sentence. See vnslides.sty rule 7.
\begin{frame}{Summary}
  This lecture located the course, argued that market participation is already
  the default for a working professional, and read five financial promises as
  schedules of dated payments.

  \vspace{0.05em}
  {\large\textbf{\ink{Key takeaways}}}
  \vspace{0.1em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.1em}
    \item \textbf{\ink{You are already a portfolio holder.}} Retirement accounts
      make market participation the default, so the allocation question is one
      nearly everyone answers, most of them by inaction.
    \item \textbf{\ink{An instrument is a schedule.}} A bill, a note, a bond, a
      corporate bond, and an inflation-indexed security differ in their dated
      cash flows, not in kind: the product names are labels for those schedules.
    \item \textbf{\ink{A claim is only as good as the rung it survived.}} Theory,
      backtest, forward test, and real money each see different data, and each
      is the only stage able to falsify its own claim.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.05em}
  On Thursday we build the rule that lets us rank those five schedules.
\end{frame}
