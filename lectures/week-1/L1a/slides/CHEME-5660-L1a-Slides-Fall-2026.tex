\begin{frame}{Objectives for Today}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  Today we answer three questions: How does the course work? Why do financial
  markets matter even if you never become a trader? What makes one investment
  preferable to another?

      investors, while companies use public markets to raise money.
    \item \hilite{Our first decision problem}: two investments pay different

\begin{frame}{Gross Notional Value Is About 108 Times Premium Paid}
  \vspace{0.05em}
  In 2025, \hilite{60.4 million} option contracts traded each day. Buyers paid
  \hilite{USD 37B} in premiums for about \hilite{USD 4T} in gross notional value:
  \[
    \underbrace{\text{USD } 4\text{T}}_{\text{\footnotesize gross notional value}}
    \;\Big/\;
    \underbrace{\text{USD } 37\text{B}}_{\text{\footnotesize premium paid}}
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{108\times}_{\text{\footnotesize about \$108 gross notional per \$1 paid}}
  \]

  \vspace{0.15em}
  About \hilite{USD 3T} came from cash-settled S\&P 500 index options. For an option, gross notional divided by premium approximates exposure-based
  leverage when $|\delta| \approx 1$.

  \slidenote{Contract size:}{equity options usually cover 100 shares. For index options, the multiplier converts index points into dollars (SPX: \$100 per point).}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Payment List Leaves Three Questions Open}
  \vspace{0.15em}
  Choosing the largest listed payment is tempting, but each row hides a
  different issue.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35em}
    \item \hilite{C: What if you need the money before year 30?} Selling early
      means accepting the market price then, and that price changes with
      interest rates. \dimmed{Week 2.}
    \item \hilite{D: Why does it pay more?} Its rate is 5.42\%, versus 4.71\%
      for the ten-year Treasury. That gap reflects the chance of missed payments
      and the difficulty of selling quickly. \dimmed{Units 2 and 3.}
    \item \hilite{E: What does inflation protection change?} Its payments rise
      with inflation, so its 2.41\% rate is not directly comparable with the
      other rates. \dimmed{Later in the course.}
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.35em}
  \ink{A larger listed payment is not automatically a better investment.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
  Financial decisions start with payment schedules, but the largest payment is
  not automatically the best choice.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  {\large\textbf{\ink{Key takeaways}}}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35em}
    \item \textbf{\ink{Markets connect investors and companies.}} Cornell
      invests its endowment; going public lets a company raise capital and its
      shareholders trade.
    \item \textbf{\ink{A product name is a label.}} What matters first is the
      payment schedule: what gets paid, when, and under what conditions.
    \item \textbf{\ink{Models earn trust one test at a time.}} Theory, backtest,
      forward test, and real money use different evidence and can reveal
      different failures.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  On Thursday, we build our first comparison rule: how to compare money paid at
  different times.
\end{frame}
