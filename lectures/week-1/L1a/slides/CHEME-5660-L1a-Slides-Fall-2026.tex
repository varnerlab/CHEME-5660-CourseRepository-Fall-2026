\begin{frame}{You Already Hold a Portfolio}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  Roughly \hilite{61\%} of U.S. adults hold a retirement account: a
  401(k), a 403(b), or an IRA. In household wealth generation, markets are
  second only to real estate. \dimmed{(2025)}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  So the question is not whether to participate. You already do.

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5em}
    \item That account holds a \hilite{portfolio}: a set of positions, in some
      proportion, chosen by someone.
    \item Choosing those proportions is an \hilite{allocation decision}, and it
      is the same decision this course spends four units learning to make.
    \item Most people holding one have \hilite{never consciously made it}. A
      default was selected for them and they left it alone.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \ink{Inaction is an allocation. It is just not an argued one.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Twelve People, Eight Meetings a Year}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  One committee of twelve meets eight times a year and moves the value of
  everything in that account.

  \vspace{1.0em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.6em}
    \item \hilite{Thursday}: you meet them. What they actually control, what
      they do not, and why that distinction decides which number you use to
      value anything.
    \item \hilite{September 15 and 16}: they meet. We are three weeks into the
      course by then, and we come back to it in week four, with the tools to
      say what the decision did.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \dimmed{That is the whole teaser. Sixty seconds, and we move on.}

  \slidenote{Link:}{\href{https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm}{FOMC calendar and meeting materials}}
\end{frame}

% Summary frames carry all three parts: one summary sentence, exactly three

\begin{frame}{Summary}
  This lecture located the course, argued that you are already a market
  participant, and read five financial promises as schedules of dated payments.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  {\large\textbf{\ink{Key takeaways}}}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35em}
    \item \textbf{\ink{You are already a portfolio holder.}} Retirement accounts
      make market participation the default, so the allocation question is one
      nearly everyone answers, most of them by inaction.
    \item \textbf{\ink{An instrument is a schedule.}} A bill, a note, a bond, a
      corporate bond, and an inflation-indexed security differ in their dated
      cash flows, not in kind: the product names are labels for those schedules.
    \item \textbf{\ink{A claim is only as good as the rung it survived.}} Theory,
      backtest, forward test, and real money each see different data, and each
      is the only stage able to falsify its own claim.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  On Thursday we build the rule that lets us rank those five schedules.
\end{frame}
