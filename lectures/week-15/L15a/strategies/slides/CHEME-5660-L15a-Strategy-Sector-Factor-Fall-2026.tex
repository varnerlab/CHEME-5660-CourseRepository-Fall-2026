\begin{frame}{The Idea}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  The sector factor estimates start from the fixed estimates and add a sector term to the expected growth rate of
  each stock:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5em}
    \item \hilite{Fixed fit}: the baseline $\hat{\alpha}_{i}$ (1/yr) and the market sensitivity $\hat{\beta}_{i}$ of
      stock $i$ keep their values from the 2016 to 2024 fit.
    \item \hilite{Sector term}: the expected growth rate adds the recent growth of the stock's sector that the
      market does not explain.
    \item \hilite{ETFs}: an exchange-traded fund (ETF) has no sector, so its expected growth rate keeps the single
      index form.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  The engine adds the sector term only to the fixed fit, because an updated baseline $\alpha_{i}(t)$ already
  follows the recent growth of each stock, including the growth of its sector.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Sector Term}
  Previously, we built the sector factor model, in which the sector factor $f_{s}(t)$ is the average residual of the
  stocks in sector $s$. On day $t$, stock $j$ has the growth rate $g_{j}(t)$ (1/yr), and \texttt{SPY} has the growth
  rate $g_{m}(t)$ with the 252-day exponential moving average (EMA) $\tilde{g}_{M,t}$. For stock $i$ in sector $s$:
  {\setlength{\jot}{1pt}
  \begin{align*}
    \varepsilon_{j}(t) &= g_{j}(t)-\hat{\alpha}_{j}-\hat{\beta}_{j}\,g_{m}(t) && \text{residual of stock } j\text{, fixed fit}\\
    f_{s,-i}(t) &= \frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\sum_{j\in s,\ j\neq i}\varepsilon_{j}(t) && \text{sector factor without stock } i\\
    \tilde{f}_{s,-i}(t) &= \text{252-day EMA of } f_{s,-i}(t) && \text{smoothed like } \tilde{g}_{M,t}\\
    \Longrightarrow\quad &\boxed{\tilde{g}_{i,t}=\hat{\alpha}_{i}+\hat{\beta}_{i}\,\tilde{g}_{M,t}+\hat{\delta}_{i}\,\tilde{f}_{s,-i}(t)}
      && \text{expected growth rate (1/yr)}
  \end{align*}}%
  where $n_{s}$ counts the stocks of sector $s$ with a price on day $t$. The loading $\hat{\delta}_{i}$ is the least
  squares slope, with an intercept, of $\varepsilon_{i}(t)$ on $f_{s,-i}(t)$ over 2016 to 2024. The preference weight
  $\gamma_{i}(t)$ keeps its \href{\repo/engine/CHEME-5660-L15a-Trading-Engine-Fall-2026.ipynb}{formula}, with
  $\tilde{g}_{i,t}$ in the numerator and $\hat{\beta}_{i}$ in the denominator.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Where the Sector Term Enters}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  At every close, the engine adds $\hat{\delta}_{i}\tilde{f}_{s,-i}(t)$ to the fixed baseline $\hat{\alpha}_{i}$. Every part
  of a decision that uses the expected growth rate then carries the sector term:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5em}
    \item \hilite{Preference weights}: the gamma picking rules, and the Cobb-Douglas, CES, Stone-Geary, and 130/30
      long-short weighting rules.
    \item \hilite{Maximum Sharpe}: the expected excess growth rates that set the weights.
    \item \hilite{Band-exit trigger}: the forecast of the wealth over the next 21 trading days.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  The market sensitivities and the covariance are the same as with fixed estimates. Minimum variance, equal
  weight, and BAB change only through the basket and the decision days.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reading the Code}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  The code file is
  \href{\engine/SectorTerm.jl}{\texttt{code/src/mpc/SectorTerm.jl}}:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5em}
    \item \texttt{MPC.sector\_factor\_series(md, calib)} computes the sector factor $f_{s,-i}(t)$ of every stock on every day.
    \item \texttt{MPC.sector\_loadings(md, calib, F; train\_start, train\_stop)} fits the loading $\hat{\delta}_{i}$ of every stock.
    \item \texttt{MPC.smoothed\_sector\_factor(F; window)} computes $\tilde{f}_{s,-i}(t)$, with \texttt{window~=~252}.
    \item \texttt{MPC.add\_sector\_term!(sim, md, calib, s)} adds $\hat{\delta}_{i}\tilde{f}_{s,-i}(t)$ to the fixed baseline on every day of the run.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  The setting \texttt{estimates~=~"sector\_factor"} selects these estimates. The
  \href{\repo/strategies/CHEME-5660-L15a-Strategy-Menu-Fall-2026.ipynb}{strategy menu} lists every rule and setting, and the
  \href{\repo/engine/CHEME-5660-L15a-Trading-Engine-Fall-2026.ipynb}{trading engine} describes the preference weight and the triggers.
\end{frame}
