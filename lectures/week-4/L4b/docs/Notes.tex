\section{From Random Walks to Positive Prices}

Brownian motion entered finance through attempts to describe continuously fluctuating prices. Louis Bachelier used an arithmetic Brownian model in 1900; later work associated with Paul Samuelson emphasized multiplicative dynamics so prices remain positive. Geometric Brownian motion (GBM) became foundational because it has an analytic solution, scales noise with price, and connects cleanly to option-pricing theory. These virtues make it a benchmark, not an empirical law.

GBM assumes the ex-dividend share price $S_t>0$ satisfies the stochastic differential equation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gbm-sde}
  dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t,
\end{equation}
where $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$ is the annual arithmetic drift in the price SDE, $\sigma>0$ is annual volatility with units years$^{-1/2}$, and $W_t$ is a standard Wiener process. We reserve $g$ for an observed continuously compounded growth rate. Both $\mu$ and $g$ have units years$^{-1}$, but they are not the same quantity.

\begin{definition}{Standard Wiener process}{wiener-process}
A standard Wiener process $(W_t)_{t\geq0}$ is a continuous real-valued stochastic process satisfying $W_0=0$ almost surely; increments over disjoint intervals are independent; and for $0\leq s<t$, the increment $W_t-W_s$ is normally distributed with mean zero and variance $t-s$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:wiener-increment}
  W_t-W_s\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{t-s}\,Z,
  \qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),
\end{equation}
where $\overset{d}{=}$ denotes equality in distribution.
\end{definition}

Independent Gaussian increments give GBM tractability and also predetermine several of its empirical failures.

\section{It\^o's Lemma Creates the Variance Correction}

Ordinary calculus would incorrectly write $d\log S_t=dS_t/S_t$. Brownian increments have quadratic variation of order $dt$, so the second derivative contributes at first order. It\^o's lemma states the required stochastic chain rule (\thmref{ito-lemma}).

\begin{theorem}{One-dimensional It\^o formula}{ito-lemma}
Let $X_t$ satisfy $dX_t=a(X_t,t)\,dt+b(X_t,t)\,dW_t$, and let $f(x,t)$ be once continuously differentiable in $t$ and twice continuously differentiable in $x$. Under standard integrability conditions, $Y_t:=f(X_t,t)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ito-lemma}
  dY_t=
  \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}
  +a\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
  +\frac{1}{2}b^2\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)dt
  +b\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dW_t.
\end{equation}
All coefficients and derivatives on the right are evaluated at $(X_t,t)$.
\end{theorem}

Apply \thmref{ito-lemma} to $f(S)=\log S$. Because $f'(S)=1/S$ and $f''(S)=-1/S^2$, the GBM dynamics imply
\begin{align}
  d\log S_t
  &=\left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)dt+\sigma\,dW_t,
  \label{eq:log-gbm}\\
  \log\!\left(\frac{S_T}{S_0}\right)
  &=\left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)T+\sigma W_T.
  \label{eq:integrated-log-gbm}
\end{align}
Exponentiation gives the exact GBM solution (\propref{gbm-solution}).

\begin{proposition}{Exact solution and distribution of GBM}{gbm-solution}
For constant $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$, $\sigma>0$, initial price $S_0>0$, and horizon $T>0$ years, the solution of \eqnref{gbm-sde} is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gbm-solution}
  S_T=S_0\exp\!\left[
  \left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)T+\sigma\sqrt{T}\,Z
  \right],
  \qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).
\end{equation}
Thus $\log(S_T/S_0)$ is normal with mean $(\mu-\sigma^2/2)T$ and variance $\sigma^2T$, while $S_T$ is lognormal and strictly positive almost surely.
\end{proposition}

The correction $-\sigma^2/2$ reconciles arithmetic and log growth. From the lognormal moments,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gbm-moments}
  \E[S_T]=S_0e^{\mu T},
  \qquad
  \Var(S_T)=S_0^2e^{2\mu T}\left(e^{\sigma^2T}-1\right).
\end{equation}
The median is $S_0e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)T}$, below the mean when $\sigma>0$ because the price distribution is right-skewed.

\section{Exact Discrete Simulation}

For a grid $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N$ with step lengths $\Delta t_j:=t_j-t_{j-1}>0$ years, GBM can be sampled exactly at grid points:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:exact-step}
  S_{t_j}=S_{t_{j-1}}
  \exp\!\left[
  \left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\Delta t_j
  +\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t_j}\,Z_j
  \right],
  \qquad Z_j\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1).
\end{equation}
This is an exact transition for constant-parameter GBM, not the Euler--Maruyama approximation. Monte Carlo repeats \eqnref{exact-step} with independent pseudorandom streams to approximate path or terminal distributions. Reproducibility requires recording the seed and generator, while uncertainty in Monte Carlo estimates should be distinguished from uncertainty in $\mu$ and $\sigma$.

\section{Estimating Drift and Volatility Correctly}

For equally spaced observations, define the growth rate $g_j:=(1/\Delta t)\log(S_{t_j}/S_{t_{j-1}})$ and its associated log return $r_j:=(\Delta t)g_j$. Under GBM,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:log-return-law}
  g_j\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
  \mathcal{N}\!\left[
  \mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2},
  \frac{\sigma^2}{\Delta t}
  \right].
\end{equation}
Write $\bar g:=\mu-\sigma^2/2$ for the mean growth rate. If $g'$ is the sample mean and $\sigma_g$ is the sample standard deviation of the growth rates (the L3a risk measure), the natural moment estimates are
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gbm-estimates}
  \widehat\sigma=\sigma_g\sqrt{\Delta t},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\bar g}=g',
  \qquad
  \widehat\mu=\widehat{\bar g}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
The sample mean $g'$ estimates the mean growth rate $\bar g=\mu-\sigma^2/2$, not the arithmetic SDE drift itself, and $\sigma_g$ estimates $\sigma/\sqrt{\Delta t}$, not $\sigma$. Omitting the half-variance adjustment, or the $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ scaling, silently changes the meaning of the model parameter.

The drift parameter is difficult to estimate over short samples because expected growth is small relative to growth-rate noise. Regressing log price on time does not remove stochastic residuals and generally creates serially correlated regression errors. Likelihood or growth-rate moment methods align more directly with \eqnref{log-return-law}, but they remain vulnerable to changing regimes, outliers, selection choices, and model misspecification.

\section{Closed-Form Target Probability}

Use the present-value return definition from L4a without costs, with $g_b$ the continuously compounded annual benchmark growth rate: $\rho_T:=e^{-g_bT}S_T/S_0-1$. For a target $\rho_\star>-1$, substitute \eqnref{gbm-solution} and standardize the normal shock. The success probability is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gbm-pop}
  \Pmeasure(\rho_T>\rho_\star)
  =1-\PhiCDF\!\left(
  \frac{\log(1+\rho_\star)-
  (\mu-g_b-\sigma^2/2)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}
  \right),
\end{equation}
where $\PhiCDF$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function and the probability uses the real-world parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma$. Replacing $\mu$ by a risk-neutral drift changes the measure and no longer answers an empirical probability-of-profit question.

\section{Which Stylized Facts Does GBM Encode?}

GBM growth rates over nonoverlapping equal-length intervals are independent Gaussian observations with constant variance rate. The model therefore produces zero autocorrelation in those growth rates, consistent with one broad stylized fact. It cannot generate heavy-tailed growth rates or volatility clustering because $\sigma$ is constant and Gaussian tails are built in. The lognormal price distribution should not be confused with a heavy-tailed growth-rate law.

GBM can still be useful for short-horizon scenarios, benchmarking, and deriving analytical intuition. Its adequacy must be judged against the decision: a model used to estimate extreme loss or dynamic hedging error demands stronger tail and volatility validation than one used to teach stochastic calculus.
